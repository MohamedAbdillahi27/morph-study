    DNA Replication

    Follow how Escherichia coli copies both strands of its DNA.

    The interactive model is unavailable.

    The exhibit text below explains the same process.

    Structure

    A double helix

    The two teal backbones run in opposite directions. Adenine pairs with thymine and guanine pairs with cytosine. Each strand can serve as a template when the cell copies its chromosome before dividing.

    Scroll to follow the fork, or select a part to look closer.

    Initiation

    Start at an origin

    Replication begins at a specific site on the circular E. coli chromosome. Two forks travel around the chromosome in opposite directions. This local view follows one fork moving down.

    Unwinding

    The fork opens

    DnaB helicase separates the strands by breaking the hydrogen bonds between paired bases. The covalent bonds within each parental backbone stay intact.

    Stabilization

    Keep the templates open

    DNA gyrase relieves twisting ahead of the fork. Single-strand binding proteins coat exposed DNA and keep the separated templates from pairing again.

    Priming

    RNA gives DNA a starting point

    DNA polymerase needs an existing 3′ end to add a base. DnaG primase makes the short RNA primers shown in violet, supplying that first 3′ end.

    Leading strand

    Follow the fork

    DNA polymerase III adds bases to the growing 3′ end of the leading strand, shown in lighter teal. The template runs 3′ to 5′ toward the fork, so the new DNA can grow continuously in that direction.

    Lagging strand

    Build in short fragments

    The opposite template faces the other way. As the fork exposes more DNA, primase lays another primer near the fork and polymerase extends it 5′ to 3′ away from the fork. Each new segment is an Okazaki fragment.

    Maturation

    From fragments to a strand

    DNA polymerase I replaces RNA primers with DNA. DNA ligase completes the sugar-phosphate backbone by forming a phosphodiester bond at each remaining nick.

    Completion

    Two daughter helices

    Each finished duplex contains one parental strand and one newly made strand. This is semiconservative replication.

    Go deeper
